A long article reads better with a map. The table of contents lists the article's sections, links to each one, and underlines the one the reader is in.

## Turn it on

In the article's sidebar, tick **Show table of contents**. It's off until you do. A preview of the list appears under the checkbox and updates as you type.

If the preview says _Add a heading to build the table of contents._, the article has nothing to list yet.

## Give a heading a shorter label

A heading like _Michigan Senate – Smith vs Jones_ reads well in the article but makes a long entry. With **Show table of contents** ticked, click into the heading: a box labelled _In the table of contents_ appears under it, with the heading's words in grey. Type a shorter label there, like _Michigan Senate_, and the table of contents shows that instead. The heading in the article stays as you wrote it, and the preview in the sidebar shows the label. Press Enter to go back to writing.

Leave the box empty to list the heading's own words. The label stays with its heading when you reword or move the heading, and goes when you delete it.

## What goes in it

- **Every heading.** H2s are the top level, H3s sit under them, and H4s under those.
- **Tables**, listed as "Table". A table placed right under a heading folds into that heading's entry instead.
- **Some blocks**: an Interactive Map (by its title), a Media Collage ("Image grid" or "Carousel"), a Social Embed (like "YouTube embed") and a Timeline (by its title).
- **Intro**, first, when the article doesn't open with a heading.

## Where readers see it

On a wide screen it sits beside the article and stays in view as the reader scrolls. On a phone it comes before the article text. A button in the article's header, next to the share buttons, collapses and expands it.

Each heading also gets a link icon on hover, so readers can copy a link straight to a section.

## Tips

- **Use real headings**, from the editor's heading options, not bold text. Bold text isn't listed.
- **Keep headings short**, or give long ones a label. They're the list's entries.
- **Changing a heading changes its link.** A section's address comes from its label, or its words when it has none (_Turnout by county_ becomes `#turnout-by-county`), so links people have shared to it stop jumping there. Adding or changing a label changes the link too.
- Two headings with the same words get `-2`, `-3` added to the second and third.

Admins can switch the table of contents off for the whole site under **Site Settings → Experiments → Article table of contents**. It's on unless someone turns it off.
